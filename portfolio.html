Storefront rebuild · 2025 · 6 weeks

Meridian Coffee Roasters

Live siteSource

Their old storefront took over four seconds to show anything on a phone, and two thirds of their traffic was phones. Carts were being abandoned at the payment step.

I rebuilt the front-end in Astro against their existing Shopify backend, cut the JavaScript bundle from 480 kB to 71 kB, and replaced the third-party image slider with native lazy-loaded sources.

Largest Contentful Paint dropped from 4.2s to 1.1s and mobile checkout completion rose 18% over the following quarter.

  • Astro
  • Shopify Storefront API
  • TypeScript
  • Core Web Vitals

Multi-location clinic site · 2024 · 4 weeks

Sahil Dental Group

Live site

Five clinics, one phone line, and a receptionist taking every booking by hand. The brief from the agency was a site that books appointments without adding software the staff had to learn.

I built the front-end and wired a booking flow to their existing calendar, then set the site up in WordPress with editable blocks so the office manager could change hours and staff photos herself. Full keyboard support and WCAG 2.2 AA contrast throughout, since many of their patients are older.

Online bookings now cover 41% of appointments, and the agency has had no content-edit tickets since handoff.

  • WordPress
  • ACF blocks
  • PHP
  • Accessibility

The booking flow, start to finish: pick a clinic, pick a slot, confirm. Every step reachable by keyboard alone — the whole thing was tested with VoiceOver before it shipped.

Rung, a date-range picker

SourceDocs

Every booking project I worked on pulled in a 40 kB date picker that screen readers could not announce properly. So I wrote a small one.

Rung is 1.9 kB gzipped, has no dependencies, follows the ARIA grid pattern, and ships unstyled so designers can make it match anything.

Around 600 GitHub stars, and in production at three studios I know of.

  • Vanilla JS
  • ARIA
  • Zero dependencies
  • Vitest

How I work

1

Read the design properly first

I go through the file and come back with the questions that always surface later anyway: empty states, long names, what happens at 320 px, which parts the client will want to edit.

2

Build in reviewable slices

A deploy preview goes up on day one and updates with every push, so the designer and the account lead see the real thing in a browser rather than waiting for a milestone.

3

Leave it maintainable

Before I call it done: cross-browser pass, keyboard pass, performance budget checked, CMS fields labelled in plain language, and a walkthrough recorded for whoever inherits it.

About

About

I started building sites for small businesses around Karachi — a dental group, a coffee roaster, a furniture importer — where there was no one else to catch the details, so I learned to catch them myself.
